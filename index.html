Topic 01
⭐ Recommended — FNT Assistant · User Trust in AI

"How do expert users decide
whether to trust and act on
AI answers in FNT Command?"

The Problem
FNT Assistant is live in Command 14.6. Expert users ask complex questions — but without source citations, confidence signals, or clear capability limits, they can't evaluate whether to act on the answer. The result: they don't act at all, or they act on wrong information. Both are costly in critical infrastructure.
Research Questions
Q1
Which UI patterns — source citations, confidence labels, capability boundaries — most improve trust calibration and task success for expert users?
Q2
How do failure modes (timeout, wrong answer, out-of-scope) affect user recovery — and what fallback flows minimize frustration in an enterprise context?
Q3
Does showing which documents the AI used reduce over-confidence in compliance-sensitive infrastructure tasks?
What FNT Gets
  • Validated pattern library for enterprise AI trust UX
  • Quantitative evidence linking transparency to task success
  • Prioritized roadmap for FNT Assistant next release
  • Figma components ready for dev handoff
Low Risk Just Shipped A/B Testable
Topic 02
FNT Command · 7-Layer Digital Twin · Research on Existing Features

"How do expert users navigate
cross-layer dependencies to answer
'what breaks if this fails?'"

The Problem
FNT's digital twin spans 7 layers — Facility → Physical → Logical → Virtualization → Applications → Services → Business Services. The highest-value tasks require traversing 4–5 layers mentally: "If this PDU fails, which customer-facing services go down?" The current UI doesn't make this traversal intuitive. Users get lost, miss dependencies, or switch between screens at exactly the moment when speed matters most.
Research Questions
Q1
Which visualization and interaction patterns best support traversing cross-layer dependencies without overwhelming expert users?
Q2
How do AI-generated impact summaries ("4 services affected, 2 customer-facing") affect decision speed and accuracy vs. manual graph traversal?
Q3
What level of detail — full dependency graph vs. plain-language impact card vs. progressive disclosure — best matches expert users' mental models during incident triage?
What FNT Gets
  • Interaction pattern set for multi-layer dependency views
  • Evidence on when AI summaries outperform graph traversal
  • Design recommendations for GraphicCenter and impact analysis
Core Product High Academic Novelty Medium Risk
Topic 03
ProcessCenter + AI · Human-in-the-Loop

"How should AI recommendations
be designed so operators approve
changes with confidence — not blind trust?"

The Problem
ProcessCenter manages infrastructure change workflows using BPMN 2.0. As AI recommendations enter these workflows, two failure modes emerge: analysis paralysis (operator freezes without enough context) and rubber-stamping (operator approves without understanding impact). Both are dangerous — decommissioning a PDU or modifying a WAN circuit can break live services.
Research Questions
Q1
Which approval UI patterns — risk summaries, affected services, rollback plans, AI confidence — most improve decision quality and speed without increasing cognitive load?
Q2
How should AI suggestions be framed to avoid automation bias — where operators approve because the AI said so, not because they understood the impact?
Q3
Does integrated impact visualization directly in the approval form reduce context-switching errors during high-stakes changes?
What FNT Gets
  • Pattern library for AI-assisted change approval UX
  • Evidence on automation bias in infrastructure operations
  • Implementation guide for ProcessCenter's AI integration
BPMN Expertise High Stakes Domain Low-Medium Risk
Topic 04
AI in Future USs · FNT Command · Forward-Looking

"What interaction patterns should
FNT establish now — before shipping
more AI features?"

The Problem
FNT is building more AI into Command — and each new AI feature ships its own UX conventions. Without a shared pattern language for AI-native interactions, users encounter inconsistent trust signals, unpredictable capability boundaries, and conflicting interaction models across features. Establishing these patterns now — before they accumulate — is far cheaper than retrofitting consistency later.
Research Questions
Q1
What core interaction patterns — capability declaration, progressive disclosure, failure recovery, handoff to human — are necessary across all AI features in enterprise infrastructure software?
Q2
How do users build mental models of AI capability across multiple features — and how does inconsistency erode overall platform trust?
Q3
Which future AI use cases (predictive capacity, auto-provisioning, anomaly explanation) require patterns that don't yet exist in enterprise UX?
What FNT Gets
  • AI interaction pattern library for FNT's design system
  • Cross-feature consistency audit with prioritized fixes
  • Foundation for all future AI feature UX decisions
FNT Nominated Highest Novelty Strategic Value
Topic 05
Infrastructure H&M · NEW Product · Alert Fatigue

"How do you design infrastructure
health views that operators act on —
instead of learning to ignore?"

The Problem
FNT Infrastructure Health & Monitoring just shipped — surfacing threshold violations, "last seen" signals, and documentation quality gaps. The risk: designing it like a monitoring system with too many signals and low actionability. Alert fatigue is well-documented in ICU monitoring and SIEM tools — when everything is flagged, nothing gets attention. A real failure gets missed even though the system flagged it. The UX is still being formed — this is the rarest situation in enterprise software.
Research Questions
Q1
Which visual encodings and aggregations — health scores, trend arrows, risk bands — best support rapid triage without overwhelming operators?
Q2
How should documentation quality indicators be presented to motivate corrective action — without creating shame, noise, or being ignored?
Q3
What handoff patterns to ProcessCenter or EventEngine minimize context switching during active incidents?
What FNT Gets
  • Pattern set for health/status visualization in infrastructure tools
  • Guidelines coupling health signals to workflows
  • Evidence-based UX direction for a product still being formed
New Product Best Timing Lowest Risk
